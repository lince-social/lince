**Access Control concerns and implementation recommendations**

Reviewed on 2026-09-13. Implementation is deferred; these notes preserve the concerns and proposed approaches for later discussion.

These notes refine the Access Control Castle in Lince.lingua. They are recommendations for discussion, not claims of finished behavior or approval of every choice. The Record remains the source of truth. The requested direction is multiple additive Roles, editable access rules, no special authority attached to the name `admin`, and a small implementation whose ordinary checks stay cheap without weakening security.

**Existing gaps and enforcement concerns**

The current backend stores one Role per Person. It has scoped write grants, revision checks and protection against indirect access changes, but some Actions still treat `admin` specially. The stricter access module and the ordinary Action and collaboration routes must be checked together; a helper and its tests do not establish enforcement at every entry point. Reuse the existing engine, Protein conditions and store rather than adding a separate permission service. Relevant code is in [access](../crates/engine/src/access.rs), [authority](../crates/protein/src/authority.rs), [Actions](../crates/engine/src/actions.rs) and [sessions](../crates/transport/src/session.rs).

**Actor identity and additive Roles**

Keep an Actor's identity separate from its credentials and devices. Role membership should refer to stable Actor and Role identities within the authority of an Organ, with duplicate memberships prevented. Decide explicitly which Actor kinds can receive Roles; the current Person-only model does not establish Agent or Organ membership. Remote login must not import the remote Organ's Role names as local authority. Avoid Role inheritance unless a concrete need appears.

Each grant keeps its own operation, Record conditions, properties and assertion rules. Apply the Role's own read restrictions and operation requirements before adding its grants to those from another Role. A broad reader Role must not widen a narrow editor Role. Exclusions narrow their own Role; they do not cancel another Role's grant. Session validity, Organ scope and other independent access limits still apply after combining Roles. Additional Roles cannot reactivate a disabled Actor or revoked device.

Recommend allowing an ordinary update when every touched property or assertion operation is covered by an applicable grant, followed by one check of the complete resulting Record. Include attempted writes that leave the same value, not only the final difference. For an update, each covering grant must match the required current and proposed state; do not borrow the current match from one Role and the proposed match from another. This deliberately changes the current requirement that one grant cover an entire Record edit. Creation, deletion, restoration and coupled domain operations need their own complete checks. Moving a Record out of its permitted category needs an explicit transition grant, not a general exception to these checks.

**Permission management and recovery concerns**

Permission names correspond to operations implemented by the backend. People can compose Roles and conditions, but inventing a permission label cannot create enforcement for a new operation. Avoid making a person configure both a broad `record:update` checkbox and a second unrelated write rule to express one choice; the Castle should show the effective rule and explain any missing prerequisite. Generic Record editing must not alter credentials, Role membership, sharing controls or domain-owned fields through an extension or assertion shortcut.

Separate assigning an existing Role from editing what that Role permits. For simple delegation, recommend an explicit list of assignable Roles, controlled by an Actor allowed to manage access for the Organ. Do not try to prove that any two arbitrary Protein filters describe a subset of one another. A delegated manager must not edit an assignable Role into a stronger one. Access management is an explicit capability; its holder need not automatically gain the right to read all business data. Preview and audit output must respect that distinction.

Save access changes with expected revisions, their dependency information and audit entries in one transaction. Check the authority that existed before the change; a request cannot grant itself the permission needed to approve itself. Role removal must remove its memberships, and restoring an Actor must not silently restore revoked credentials or devices. Protect an actual usable recovery route instead of counting Roles named `admin`. Changing a Role name must not change access. Recovery must work even when ordinary read rules are broken.

**Filter concerns and recommendations**

Access rules share Protein's supported conditions and controls, but save their own definition. Validate the entire rule before accepting it, including unused branches, referenced identities and work limits. Missing references are errors, including beneath `not`; they must not become broad access. Make exact Concept matching and matching descendants distinguishable where supported. Sorting, pagination and the choice of fields shown in a Sand do not belong to the rule. Conditions based on changing time need explicit expiry handling; do not accidentally obtain them by accepting every display filter.

**Performance concerns and recommendations**

The current [metadata snapshot](../crates/store/src/access_snapshot.rs) scans complete metadata tables and defaults to 4,096 Records. Write preparation also reads all saved Role policies and personal filters. This makes ordinary work depend on unrelated database growth, and an invalid unrelated policy can stop preparation. Raising these limits or removing the checks does not fix that design.

Recommend caching validated rule structure by policy revision, with bounded memory. Do not initially cache final decisions such as “Actor A may edit Record B.” Read current memberships, session state and relevant Record data from the same database state used for the operation. Cache entries must retain the Role restrictions and conditions, not only a list of writable property names. Rule structure that contains resolved Concept families or graph results also needs their revisions; caching only syntax is simpler.

Use indexed lookups for the target Records and the relationships their rules need. Batch those lookups for a request and share rule preparation between targets. Existing assertion indexes already cover subject and object lookups. Bound the actual request, rule size, visited relationships and returned data rather than rejecting every operation once the whole Organ passes a Record count. A bounded graph walk must detect exhaustion and refuse the affected operation, never treat an incomplete walk as proof that no restriction exists.

Save which fields, Concepts and relationship kinds each policy depends on. This derived lookup must be updated atomically with the policy and be rebuildable. Ordinary title edits can then avoid unrelated hierarchy checks when no policy depends on the title. Dependency tracking must include newly inserted relationships, removed links and changes to Concept ancestry, not just Record IDs that matched previously. Preserve the current protection against changing other Records' access indirectly: check affected conditions before and after a structural change. Invalidating a cache alone does not authorize that change. If the affected scope cannot be established safely within the work limit, refuse the change with a reason.

This does not require storing every Actor–Record permission pair or recalculating every Actor after every edit. Share validated rule structure, resolve current data when needed, and index policy dependencies. Begin with the smallest supported deterministic condition set that meets the selected workflows. Do not add another condition evaluator with different meanings for previews and real writes.

The engine currently has an access lock that separates writes from reads. Keep transactions and work under that lock short, with no network waits, interface waits or password hashing. Measure contention before changing the locking model. Correct transaction checks must remain effective across database connections; an in-process lock alone is insufficient. Ordinary typing should not repeatedly parse all policies or inspect the whole graph.

**Read privacy, preview and revocation concerns**

The related Record task also mentions property-level reads. The current Role policy selects whole Records, while write grants name properties. Field privacy needs backend filtering of queries, exports, history and collaboration payloads. The existing shared title/body document makes independent privacy for those fields a separate storage and sync decision. Hiding the description control or omitting it from an ordinary Protein result does not protect a description still present in a document snapshot. Keep this requirement visible rather than claiming display settings implement it.

Apply read authorization before pagination, counts and aggregates. Check related Records, attachments, history and presence as well as the main row. The access preview uses the same decision code and returns reasons the viewer may see, without exposing hidden values or private Role details. For large changes, show a bounded sample and clearly label whether counts are exact; do not scan the whole Organ on every filter keystroke. A preview does not authorize a later save, which must recheck current revisions and authority.

After a committed revocation, new writes and new outbound reads must use the new authority. Queued subscriptions need a generation check or re-evaluation before dispatch, with ordering defined against revocation. Cancel stale subscriptions and refresh affected views; a missed notification must not leave a stale allow decision in use. Already delivered bytes cannot be recalled. Pending edits stay attached to their original Actor and Organ and must not be retried under a different login. A local owner session is a separate execution context: changing the Actor shown in the Castle cannot turn that session into a trustworthy preview of restricted access.

The Castle should show an Actor's Roles, effective access and the rule behind each result, with direct controls for assignment, rule editing and revocation. Keep broad impact previews out of the normal edit path. The server should supply supported operations and reasons so the interface does not grow its own permission engine.

**Recommended verification for a future implementation**

Measure simple checks with 1,000, 10,000 and 100,000 unrelated Records while keeping the target and its relationships fixed, then separately grow relevant relationships and Role counts. Track rows visited, database calls, rule preparation, memory and latency under simultaneous reads and writes. Include a database above the current 4,096-Record limit, an idle Castle and revocation during queued delivery. Security checks should cover broad-reader plus narrow-editor Roles, combined property edits, category transitions, missing references under negation, new graph links, stale saves, credential-free Actors, restore behavior and rejected partial batches. Compare optimized decisions with a straightforward evaluator on generated small graphs. No performance or security result is claimed until these checks run.
