Iroh transport qualification for ESP32-S3

iroh 1.3.0: n0 computer and contributors, MIT OR Apache-2.0.
https://github.com/n0-computer/iroh
The bundled BSD-3-Clause notice covers the included upstream transport code.

rusty_esp_iroh-host 0.1.1: Remade With Rust contributors, MIT OR Apache-2.0.
https://github.com/Remade-With-Rust/rusty_esp_iroh
Its published crypto-provider adapter and relay::StdDnsResolver are used. These
carry n0 computer's ESP32 QUIC adapter and lightweight system DNS integration.
The relay::apply and skip_verify helpers are not used; infrastructure TLS keeps
normal certificate verification.

ESP-IDF 5.5.2 supplies Mbed TLS 3.6.5 for device ECDSA signature verification
through the shared lince_core binding. Rustls retains certificate chain, issuer,
name and time policy. The SDK-specific implementation and hardware MPI routines
are reused; Lince does not implement elliptic-curve arithmetic. Its selected
Apache-2.0 license and SDK notices accompany the shared C export in ../licenses/.
The diagnostic's public verification vectors come from RFC6979 A.2.5/A.2.6.
https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6979.html

rustls 0.23.44: rustls contributors, Apache-2.0 OR ISC OR MIT.
https://github.com/rustls/rustls

The selected provider uses rustls-rustcrypto-gated 0.0.2-alpha, the published
feature-gated upstream RustCrypto provider. P-256, P-384 and RSA verification
are enabled for infrastructure certificates. Its upstream warns
against production use. This project is a qualification candidate, not a
validated replacement for the installed camera firmware. Peer authentication,
infrastructure certificate verification, Xtensa compilation, direct/relay
interoperation and simultaneous camera operation must be established before
enabling this transport for the enrolled device.

Wi-Fi credentials and private keys must be provisioned on-device, never embedded
in these sources. Selected upstream license texts accompany exports in licenses/.
