Chapter 5 — People

Cells & Organs

Everything so far happened in one place: your Cell. A Cell is your own Lince — your records, on your machine, answering to you. 

graph LR
  A["Cell"]

Other people have their own. No Cell is inside any other, and none of them is the "main" one: 

graph LR
  A["Cell"]
  B["Cell"]
  C["Cell"]
  D["Cell"]

When several Cells want to hold something in common — a household budget, a team backlog, a shared shopping list — they form an Organ. 

graph TD
  O["Organ"]
  A["Cell"] --> O
  B["Cell"] --> O
  C["Cell"] --> O
  D["Cell"] --> O

An Organ is a shared context, not a server that owns the members.

You belong to as many Organs as you have contexts, and they do not blur into each other. Work does not see your hobby; your family does not see your work backlog. 

graph TD
  B["Work"] --> A["Your Cell"]
  C["Hobby"] --> A
  D["Family"] --> A
  E["Friends"] --> A

Lince as a whole is just all of it — Cells and Organs, with no centre: 

graph TD
  A["Organ"] --> O["Lince"]
  B["Cell"] --> O
  C["Organ"] --> O
  D["Cell"] --> O

This is why the frontend keeps areas (in the workspace menu) separate from each other, and why a Protein can filter by organ. The boundary is real, and it is the same boundary the next chapter has to cross.
