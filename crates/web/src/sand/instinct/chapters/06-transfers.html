Chapter 6 — Between people

Transfers

A Need in your Cell and a Contribution in someone else's do not meet by themselves. A Transfer is how the two sides agree to move something across the boundary. 

graph LR
  A["Person A"] --> C["1 unit of currency"]
  B["Person B"] --> D["2 apples"]
  C <--> D

A trade: each side has what the other is short of.

Nothing about that picture is special-cased for money. Lince has no money in it. A currency is an ordinary record used as a unit, and converting between currencies is a rule you write — exactly like converting hours to days. A trade of apples for hours needs no new machinery.

It does not have to be even

A Transfer where only one side moves is a donation. A Transfer whose other side is enormous, or vague, or far away, is a request: 

graph LR
  A["Person A: 1 unit"] --> B["Person B: a brazillion apples"]

Wildly lopsided, and still a perfectly valid Transfer to record.

And it does not have to be two-sided at all:

graph LR
  A["A"] --> B["B"]
  B --> C["C"]
  C --> A

A three-party loop: nobody trades with one partner, everyone is settled.

What a Transfer actually is

A Transfer bundles promises — each one saying who owes what to whom, in which unit, by when. Its status is derived from the states of those promises rather than set by hand, which is why the Transfer sand can tell you what stage a deal is at without anyone maintaining a status field.

  • Terms are versioned: a Transfer's signed revision is part of what everyone agreed to.
  • Participants have roles — creator, participant, invitee, observer — and a Protein can filter by yours.
  • Settlement moves the quantities on both sides at once, leaving facts behind on each.

The same shape covers a marketplace purchase, splitting a bill, planning a party, or a project where four people each bring something different. It is one mechanism because it was one idea all along: a Need on one side, a Contribution on the other.
