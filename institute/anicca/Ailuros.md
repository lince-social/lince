
Do these tasks in order:

- [ ] Choose the first camera, projector and working surface. Record their interfaces, operating system, resolution, mounting, working distance, coverage and ambient light. Plan power, cooling and a stable mounting arrangement before designing an enclosure. Choose alignment and response-time targets from actual measurements.
- [ ] Bring up the camera and projector on the workstation. Show only the Lince content assigned to the surface, with controls to blank output and stop camera input from the normal computer interface. Connect any required hardware driver through Lince's existing local adapter. Blanking should keep a black output window; closing it can reveal the desktop behind it.
- [ ] Verify that the real devices enumerate through the chosen local driver and that the dedicated output window appears on the intended display without unrelated desktop content. Test Stop all capture and Blank output on the connected equipment, measuring how long a blocking camera driver takes to stop. Record the exact equipment, resolution, platform and setup.
- [ ] Measure and calibrate the first surface. Save its dimensions and position in meters, and match the camera and projector corners to it. Check points inside the surface as well as its corners. Recalibrate when equipment moves. Moving a virtual Room around the Lince canvas must not change this physical alignment.
- [ ] Record numerical corner and interior alignment errors against the agreed targets. Use the calibration pattern and face-on output preview to inspect upright walls without rotating their real pose. Verify saved alignment survives a software restart while device connections and camera capture still require deliberate reconnection.
- [ ] Show the same task or Kanban on the computer and the real surface. Change a Record's quantity and see its WGSL effect update in both places. Check text size, contrast, focus and glow under the actual lighting. A projected image of a 3D sphere is still an image on a surface, not a free-floating hologram.
- [ ] Validate a legible cleaning indicator under the actual lighting, using a bright outline or another visible treatment where appropriate. Projected darkness cannot make a bright physical floor black.
- [ ] Request a real camera frame and deliberately associate the object being discussed with a Record. Make it possible to correct or remove that association. Measure how long projection must be blanked before capture, or mask projected regions, so our own projected text is not mistaken for independent evidence. Images go to Fiote only through an explicit request; a camera observation must not automatically change a Record or complete a task.
- [ ] Verify optical separation on the equipment: the existing blank/capture actions alone do not prove projected content has disappeared before exposure. Establish deliberate object association and correction before adding automatic recognition, OCR, voice or persistent identity across camera views. A model judging that the room looks clean must not automatically complete its task.
- [ ] Choose and implement a reliable way to point, select and release on the surface, using a marker, pointing device, touch sensor or deliberate gesture suited to the equipment. Feed those interactions through the existing Lince connection. Test moving a placement, changing a Kanban task and Undo, with keyboard and mouse recovery. Tracking loss, low confidence, recalibration or disconnect must cancel an unfinished action. Use a stationary layout for the first test; later canvas changes, including physics movement, can correctly invalidate an Undo receipt.
- [ ] Implement the detector as a producer of the observation/interaction contract in `crates/nucleus/src/physical_workspace.rs`, using the paired local protocol in `crates/interface/src/native/physical_workspace/adapter.rs`. Use `crates/sandbox/examples/physical_adapter_client.rs` as the synthetic-input and JPEG-submission reference; add real detection that emits deliberate begin/update/end/cancel interactions with explicit selection and a clear release/commit action. Existing hand-model facilities may be reusable, but an open-hand position alone does not establish touch or permission to commit.
- [ ] Measure targeting accuracy, commit accuracy and capture-to-feedback latency against the agreed targets. Include beam occlusion, target deletion and reconnect in failure tests, and verify unfinished actions cancel. Confirm accidental or uncertain input does not mutate Records and that source observations and accepted results can be traced.
- [ ] Run two useful Fiote requests at the same time with the chosen model providers. Keep each request's image, Record, conversation and output destination separate. Check waiting questions, stale answers and individual cancellation. Pointing and rendering must remain responsive while Fiotes work; do not request model work for every camera frame.
- [ ] Confirm answers reach the intended conversations and surfaces with explicitly scoped frames. Measure model response delay separately from pointer feedback, and verify rendering and input retain capacity while both requests progress.
- [ ] After the first complete loop works reliably, add cameras and projectors one at a time. Keep calibration, timing, connection status and surface ownership separate for each device. Test duplicate observations, overlap, reconnect and losing one device without damaging the others. Start with one projector per surface; add blending, automatic handoff or network transport only when the setup needs them.
- [ ] Establish physical clock alignment and synchronization across devices; software support for multiple devices does not establish those properties. Refine brightness/color matching and overlapping coverage where needed. Confirm partial failure causes no crossed destinations, duplicate actions, corruption of unrelated surfaces or unbounded backlog.
- [ ] Measure the combined setup in normal use and an optimized run. Record alignment error, pointing accuracy, frame times, camera-to-pointer delay, Record-to-projection delay, Fiote response time and resource use. Tune effects, display resolution, capture rate and request limits together. Document the exact equipment, setup, failures and recovery steps as a reproducible Trail in Lince.
- [ ] Include frame-time percentiles, queue lengths and CPU/GPU/memory use in the combined-workload measurements. The earlier combined virtual-room debug replay measured 224–270 ms median frame times on shared Intel Iris Xe/Vulkan hardware; neither that replay nor a functional software demonstration establishes smooth physical-workstation performance. Profile and tune the chosen installation itself.

Room scans and room-wide effects can follow separately. Begin with measured surfaces for reliable placement and occlusion. Automatic recognition, OCR, voice and identity across camera views need their own refinement. Free-floating holography, mobile aiming and a compact lightbulb enclosure remain later research and hardware projects; they must not delay the fixed camera/projector workstation.

- [ ] Validate optional Gaussian room scans using a real scene, measuring quality and performance independently of the useful fixed-surface workstation. Check arbitrary transparent-mesh/splat ordering with visual evidence and use measured opaque surfaces or proxies where reliable occlusion matters.
- [ ] Refine room-wide cleaning/weather effects, reconstruction and sophisticated transparency around an actual scene, keeping them independent of the first surface milestone.
